## Executive Summary

This document establishes the **Conflict Recognition Architecture** for the ACRRM Practice Manager Decision Tree. Unlike traditional decision support tools that present simplified "best practice" recommendations, this architecture explicitly surfaces the tensions and conflicts that arise when the four competing priorities (NTCER Compliance, Training Quality, Business Sustainability, and Registrar Experience/Retention) clash. The architecture provides Practice Managers with frameworks for ethical and strategic decision-making when trade-offs are necessary.

**Critical Design Principle:** Conflicts are not failures—they are inherent features of complex systems. The decision tree helps Practice Managers recognize conflicts early, understand their implications, and make informed trade-off decisions that align with their practice values and strategic priorities.

## 1. Conflict Typology

The architecture identifies **six primary conflict types** based on which priority layers are in tension:

### Conflict Type 1: Compliance vs Business Sustainability
**Description:** Regulatory requirements create financial burden or operational complexity that threatens practice viability.

**Common Manifestations:**
- NTCER-mandated employment terms exceed practice budget
- Mandatory superannuation or leave entitlements strain cash flow
- Accreditation requirements necessitate infrastructure investment

**Strategic Question:** *"How do we meet our regulatory obligations without compromising our financial sustainability?"*

---

### Conflict Type 2: Training Quality vs Operational Efficiency
**Description:** Educational requirements reduce practice productivity, revenue, or workflow efficiency.

**Common Manifestations:**
- Quarantining teaching time reduces billable hours
- Patient load limits decrease practice revenue
- Supervisor time for assessments and reports diverts from clinical work

**Strategic Question:** *"How do we deliver high-quality training without undermining our operational efficiency?"*

---

### Conflict Type 3: Registrar Experience vs Practice Capacity
**Description:** Registrar support needs or expectations exceed available practice resources.

**Common Manifestations:**
- Registrar requests on-site supervision but practice can only provide off-site
- Registrar requires extensive orientation support but practice has limited administrative time
- Registrar expects flexible rostering but practice has rigid operational needs

**Strategic Question:** *"How do we support registrar wellbeing and satisfaction within our capacity constraints?"*

---

### Conflict Type 4: Compliance vs Training Quality
**Description:** Minimum compliance standards may not achieve optimal educational outcomes.

**Common Manifestations:**
- Additional Supervisor model meets compliance but reduces teaching continuity
- Minimum leave entitlement satisfies NTCER but limits registrar's educational opportunities
- Off-site supervision meets accreditation standards but reduces registrar support

**Strategic Question:** *"Should we exceed minimum compliance standards to improve training quality, and at what cost?"*

---

### Conflict Type 5: Business Sustainability vs Registrar Retention
**Description:** Practice financial constraints limit ability to provide retention-supporting initiatives.

**Common Manifestations:**
- Practice cannot afford wellbeing programs or community integration support
- Practice cannot offer post-Fellowship partnership due to financial limitations
- Practice cannot provide flexible work arrangements that registrar desires

**Strategic Question:** *"How do we balance our financial constraints with our commitment to registrar retention?"*

---

### Conflict Type 6: Compliance vs Registrar Experience
**Description:** Regulatory requirements create administrative burden or rigidity that negatively impacts registrar experience.

**Common Manifestations:**
- Mandatory reporting requirements create bureaucratic burden for registrar
- NTCER employment terms are less flexible than registrar prefers
- Accreditation requirements impose constraints on registrar autonomy

**Strategic Question:** *"How do we navigate regulatory requirements while maintaining a positive registrar experience?"*

## 2. Conflict Detection Nodes

The decision tree includes **explicit conflict detection nodes** at decision points where competing priorities are likely to clash. These nodes are distinct from standard decision nodes and serve to surface tensions before Practice Managers commit to a course of action.

### Conflict Detection Node Structure

```
⚠️ CONFLICT DETECTION NODE: [Title]
├── CONFLICT TYPE: [Type 1-6]
├── COMPETING PRIORITIES:
│   ├── Priority A: [Description and requirements]
│   └── Priority B: [Description and requirements]
├── TENSION ANALYSIS:
│   ├── Why These Priorities Conflict: [Explanation]
│   ├── Typical Manifestations: [How conflict appears in practice]
│   └── Stakeholder Perspectives: [How different stakeholders view the conflict]
├── TRADE-OFF FRAMEWORK:
│   ├── Option 1: Prioritize [Priority A] → Consequences for [Priority B]
│   ├── Option 2: Prioritize [Priority B] → Consequences for [Priority A]
│   ├── Option 3: Compromise Solution → Partial satisfaction of both priorities
│   └── Option 4: Escalate for External Support → Engage TPA, ACRRM, or other stakeholders
├── DECISION SUPPORT QUESTIONS:
│   ├── [Question 1 to help Practice Manager assess their priorities]
│   ├── [Question 2 to help Practice Manager assess their constraints]
│   └── [Question 3 to help Practice Manager assess their values]
├── STAKEHOLDER ENGAGEMENT TRIGGERS:
│   └── [When to consult TPA, Practice Owner, Supervisor, Registrar, or other parties]
└── ESCALATION PATHWAYS:
    └── [When conflict cannot be resolved internally and requires external intervention]
```

## 3. Conflict Detection Node Examples

### Example 1: Conflict Detection Node K.1.1 - Teaching Time Protection vs Revenue Generation

**⚠️ CONFLICT DETECTION NODE:** Teaching Time Protection vs Revenue Generation

**CONFLICT TYPE:** Type 2 (Training Quality vs Operational Efficiency)

**COMPETING PRIORITIES:**
- **Priority A (Training Quality):** ACRRM requires formal and informal teaching time to be protected, with patient load limits to ensure registrars receive adequate educational support.
- **Priority B (Business Sustainability):** Practice revenue depends on billable hours; quarantining teaching time reduces the number of patients that can be seen, directly impacting practice income.

**TENSION ANALYSIS:**

**Why These Priorities Conflict:**  
Teaching time is non-billable. Every hour dedicated to formal teaching or reduced patient load is an hour of foregone revenue. For practices operating on tight margins, this can create significant financial pressure.

**Typical Manifestations:**
- Practice Owner questions the financial viability of hosting a registrar due to lost revenue
- Supervisor feels pressure to see more patients and reduce teaching time
- Registrar perceives that teaching time is frequently interrupted or deprioritized

**Stakeholder Perspectives:**
- **Registrar:** Expects protected teaching time as a core component of training quality
- **Supervisor:** Feels torn between educational responsibilities and practice productivity expectations
- **Practice Owner:** Concerned about financial sustainability and return on investment in training post
- **TPA/ACRRM:** Expects practice to meet educational standards regardless of financial impact

**TRADE-OFF FRAMEWORK:**

**Option 1: Prioritize Training Quality (Strict Teaching Time Protection)**
- ✅ Consequences for Training Quality: Registrar receives high-quality education, supervisor can focus on teaching
- ⚠️ Consequences for Business Sustainability: Practice revenue decreases, may require offsetting with funding or reduced expenses
- **When to Choose:** Practice has sufficient funding buffer or can access GP Training Incentive Payments to offset lost revenue

**Option 2: Prioritize Business Sustainability (Minimal Teaching Time)**
- ⚠️ Consequences for Training Quality: Registrar may receive inadequate teaching, educational outcomes compromised
- ✅ Consequences for Business Sustainability: Practice maintains revenue, financial viability protected
- **When to Choose:** Practice is in financial distress and cannot afford revenue loss (but consider whether training post should be maintained)

**Option 3: Compromise Solution (Flexible Teaching Time Model)**
- ⚖️ Consequences for Both Priorities: Establish minimum protected teaching time (e.g., 1 hour formal teaching per week) and supplement with informal teaching during clinical sessions; registrar patient load is managed but not severely restricted
- **When to Choose:** Practice needs to balance both priorities and can negotiate a sustainable middle ground

**Option 4: Escalate for External Support**
- Engage TPA to discuss funding options (Flexible Funds for locum coverage during teaching time)
- Consult ACRRM about minimum acceptable teaching time standards
- Discuss with Practice Owner about long-term value of training post (future workforce, reputation, accreditation benefits)
- **When to Choose:** Conflict cannot be resolved internally or practice needs external validation/support

**DECISION SUPPORT QUESTIONS:**

1. **Assess Your Financial Position:** Can your practice afford to protect teaching time without jeopardizing financial viability? Have you accessed all available funding (GP Training Incentive Payments, Flexible Funds)?

2. **Assess Your Training Commitment:** Is your practice genuinely committed to delivering high-quality training, or is the training post primarily a financial arrangement? If teaching time cannot be protected, should you reconsider hosting a registrar?

3. **Assess Your Long-Term Strategy:** Does hosting a registrar align with your long-term workforce planning? Could this registrar become a future practice partner, offsetting short-term revenue loss with long-term stability?

**STAKEHOLDER ENGAGEMENT TRIGGERS:**
- Consult **Practice Owner** if teaching time protection threatens financial viability
- Consult **Supervisor** to assess realistic teaching time availability
- Consult **TPA** if funding support is needed to offset revenue loss
- Consult **Registrar** to understand educational expectations and negotiate flexible arrangements if necessary

**ESCALATION PATHWAYS:**
- If practice cannot protect minimum teaching time, escalate to TPA to discuss whether placement should be reconsidered
- If Practice Owner refuses to support teaching time protection, escalate to ACRRM for guidance on accreditation implications

### Example 2: Conflict Detection Node I.1.1 - NTCER Compliance vs Administrative Burden

**⚠️ CONFLICT DETECTION NODE:** NTCER Compliance vs Administrative Burden

**CONFLICT TYPE:** Type 6 (Compliance vs Registrar Experience)

**COMPETING PRIORITIES:**
- **Priority A (NTCER Compliance):** NTCER 2026.1 mandates use of GPSA/GPRA employment templates with specific clauses, reporting requirements, and administrative processes.
- **Priority B (Registrar Experience):** Registrars may perceive NTCER compliance processes as bureaucratic, inflexible, or administratively burdensome, negatively impacting their experience.

**TENSION ANALYSIS:**

**Why These Priorities Conflict:**  
Regulatory compliance often requires standardized processes and documentation that may feel rigid or impersonal to registrars. While compliance protects registrar rights and ensures fair employment terms, it can also create administrative friction.

**Typical Manifestations:**
- Registrar frustrated by lengthy employment contract and mandatory clauses
- Registrar perceives reporting requirements as excessive paperwork
- Registrar requests flexible employment terms that are not NTCER-compliant

**Stakeholder Perspectives:**
- **Registrar:** Wants streamlined, flexible employment arrangements with minimal bureaucracy
- **Practice Manager:** Must ensure compliance to maintain accreditation and funding eligibility
- **TPA/ACRRM:** Expects strict adherence to NTCER to protect registrar rights and training standards

**TRADE-OFF FRAMEWORK:**

**Option 1: Prioritize NTCER Compliance (Strict Adherence)**
- ✅ Consequences for Compliance: Practice maintains accreditation, funding eligibility, and legal protection
- ⚠️ Consequences for Registrar Experience: Registrar may perceive practice as inflexible or bureaucratic
- **When to Choose:** Always—compliance is non-negotiable. However, can be paired with clear communication to improve registrar understanding.

**Option 2: Prioritize Registrar Experience (Flexible Arrangements)**
- ⚠️ Consequences for Compliance: Risk of non-compliance, loss of accreditation, funding ineligibility
- ⚠️ Consequences for Registrar Experience: Short-term satisfaction may be followed by long-term negative consequences if employment rights are not protected
- **When to Choose:** Never—non-compliance is not an acceptable option.

**Option 3: Compromise Solution (Compliance with Clear Communication)**
- ⚖️ Consequences for Both Priorities: Maintain strict NTCER compliance but invest time in explaining the rationale to registrar, emphasizing how compliance protects their rights and supports their training
- **When to Choose:** Always—this is the recommended approach.

**Option 4: Escalate for External Support**
- Engage TPA to provide registrar with orientation on NTCER requirements and their benefits
- Request ACRRM resources that explain NTCER compliance in registrar-friendly language
- **When to Choose:** Registrar is resistant to compliance processes and needs external validation

**DECISION SUPPORT QUESTIONS:**

1. **Assess Your Communication:** Have you clearly explained to the registrar why NTCER compliance is necessary and how it protects their rights?

2. **Assess Registrar Concerns:** Is the registrar's frustration with compliance processes masking deeper concerns about the placement (e.g., workload, support, cultural fit)?

3. **Assess Your Processes:** Are your administrative processes as streamlined as possible while maintaining compliance, or is there unnecessary bureaucracy that could be reduced?

**STAKEHOLDER ENGAGEMENT TRIGGERS:**
- Consult **TPA** if registrar is resistant to NTCER compliance and needs external explanation
- Consult **Registrar** to understand specific concerns and address them within compliant framework
- Consult **Practice Owner** if compliance processes are creating administrative burden for practice

**ESCALATION PATHWAYS:**
- If registrar refuses to accept NTCER-compliant employment terms, escalate to TPA for mediation
- If practice is struggling with administrative burden of compliance, escalate to ACRRM for process improvement resources

## 4. Conflict Resolution Decision Support Framework

When Practice Managers encounter a conflict detection node, the decision tree provides a structured framework for resolving the conflict:

### Step 1: Acknowledge the Conflict
**Action:** Explicitly recognize that competing priorities are in tension.  
**Output:** Practice Manager understands that there is no "perfect" solution—trade-offs are necessary.

### Step 2: Assess Priority Hierarchy
**Action:** Determine which priorities are non-negotiable (e.g., compliance) and which have flexibility.  
**Output:** Practice Manager identifies which priorities must be protected and which can be compromised.

**Priority Hierarchy Guidance:**
1. **Non-Negotiable:** NTCER Compliance (regulatory requirement)
2. **High Priority:** Training Quality (core purpose of training post)
3. **High Priority:** Business Sustainability (practice must remain viable)
4. **Important:** Registrar Experience/Retention (supports long-term workforce outcomes)

**Note:** While compliance is non-negotiable, the *other three priorities* can be balanced based on practice context and values.

### Step 3: Evaluate Trade-Off Options
**Action:** Review the trade-off framework provided in the conflict detection node.  
**Output:** Practice Manager understands the consequences of each option.

### Step 4: Engage Stakeholders
**Action:** Consult relevant stakeholders (Practice Owner, Supervisor, Registrar, TPA) as indicated by stakeholder engagement triggers.  
**Output:** Practice Manager gathers input to inform decision.

### Step 5: Make Informed Decision
**Action:** Select the option that best aligns with practice values, constraints, and strategic priorities.  
**Output:** Practice Manager commits to a course of action with full understanding of trade-offs.

### Step 6: Document and Communicate
**Action:** Document the decision, rationale, and trade-offs; communicate to relevant stakeholders.  
**Output:** Transparency and accountability are maintained.

### Step 7: Monitor and Adjust
**Action:** Monitor the outcomes of the decision and adjust if negative consequences emerge.  
**Output:** Practice Manager can course-correct if the chosen option proves unsustainable.

## 5. Escalation Pathways

When conflicts cannot be resolved internally, the decision tree provides escalation pathways to external support:

### Escalation Pathway 1: TPA Support
**When to Use:** Conflict involves placement decisions, funding access, or registrar-practice disputes

**Escalation Process:**
1. Contact TPA (Training Provider Administrator) via designated contact
2. Provide context: nature of conflict, options considered, stakeholder input
3. Request: Mediation, funding support, policy clarification, or placement reconsideration
4. Document TPA guidance and integrate into decision-making

**Example Scenarios:**
- Practice cannot afford to protect teaching time → Request Flexible Funds for locum coverage
- Registrar disputes employment terms → Request TPA mediation
- Practice capacity exceeded → Request TPA support for placement deferral

---

### Escalation Pathway 2: ACRRM Support
**When to Use:** Conflict involves accreditation standards, curriculum requirements, or training quality concerns

**Escalation Process:**
1. Contact ACRRM via designated support channels
2. Provide context: nature of conflict, compliance concerns, quality implications
3. Request: Policy clarification, accreditation guidance, or training resources
4. Document ACRRM guidance and integrate into decision-making

**Example Scenarios:**
- Uncertainty about minimum teaching time standards → Request ACRRM clarification
- Conflict between accreditation requirements and practice capacity → Request ACRRM guidance
- Supervisor accreditation questions → Request ACRRM support

---

### Escalation Pathway 3: Practice Owner/Board Consultation
**When to Use:** Conflict involves financial sustainability, strategic direction, or major practice decisions

**Escalation Process:**
1. Prepare briefing for Practice Owner/Board: nature of conflict, financial implications, strategic considerations
2. Present options with trade-off analysis
3. Request: Decision authority, financial support, or strategic guidance
4. Document Practice Owner/Board decision and implement

**Example Scenarios:**
- Training post threatens financial viability → Request Practice Owner decision on continuation
- Major infrastructure investment required for accreditation → Request Board approval
- Long-term workforce planning needed → Request strategic guidance

---

### Escalation Pathway 4: External Mediation or Legal Advice
**When to Use:** Conflict involves legal liability, formal complaints, or irresolvable disputes

**Escalation Process:**
1. Engage external mediator or legal advisor
2. Provide documentation of conflict, attempts at resolution, and stakeholder positions
3. Request: Mediation, legal opinion, or dispute resolution
4. Implement mediated agreement or legal guidance

**Example Scenarios:**
- Registrar files formal complaint → Engage external mediator
- Employment dispute with legal implications → Seek legal advice
- Accreditation dispute with ACRRM → Engage formal dispute resolution process

## 6. Conflict Anticipation and Prevention

The decision tree includes **conflict anticipation nodes** at key decision points to help Practice Managers prevent conflicts before they arise:

### Conflict Anticipation Node Structure

```
🔮 CONFLICT ANTICIPATION NODE: [Title]
├── POTENTIAL CONFLICT: [Description of conflict that may arise]
├── EARLY WARNING SIGNS:
│   ├── [Sign 1]
│   ├── [Sign 2]
│   └── [Sign 3]
├── PREVENTIVE ACTIONS:
│   ├── [Action 1 to prevent conflict]
│   ├── [Action 2 to prevent conflict]
│   └── [Action 3 to prevent conflict]
└── MONITORING INDICATORS:
    └── [Metrics to track to detect conflict early]
```

### Example: Conflict Anticipation Node E.1.1 - Placement Capacity Assessment

**🔮 CONFLICT ANTICIPATION NODE:** Placement Capacity Assessment

**POTENTIAL CONFLICT:** Accepting a registrar when practice capacity is at limit may create conflicts between Training Quality and Practice Capacity (Type 3).

**EARLY WARNING SIGNS:**
- Supervisor expresses concern about time availability
- Practice workload has increased recently
- Existing registrar(s) report feeling under-supported
- Practice Owner expresses financial concerns

**PREVENTIVE ACTIONS:**
1. **Conduct Honest Capacity Assessment:** Before accepting placement, assess supervisor time, practice workload, and financial capacity realistically.
2. **Negotiate Delayed Start Date:** If capacity is temporarily constrained, negotiate with TPA for delayed start to allow practice to prepare.
3. **Engage Practice Owner Early:** Discuss financial implications and secure commitment before accepting placement.
4. **Set Clear Expectations with Registrar:** During Mutual Fit process, be transparent about practice capacity and support availability.

**MONITORING INDICATORS:**
- Supervisor time logs (are they consistently over-committed?)
- Practice revenue trends (is financial pressure increasing?)
- Registrar feedback (are they reporting adequate support?)
- TPA feedback (are reporting requirements being met on time?)

## 7. Ethical Decision-Making Framework

When conflicts involve ethical considerations (e.g., fairness to registrar vs practice sustainability), the decision tree provides an ethical decision-making framework:

### Ethical Principles for Conflict Resolution

1. **Transparency:** Be honest with all stakeholders about constraints and trade-offs.
2. **Fairness:** Ensure decisions do not unfairly disadvantage registrars or practice staff.
3. **Accountability:** Document decisions and rationale; be prepared to justify choices.
4. **Beneficence:** Prioritize decisions that support registrar development and rural workforce outcomes where possible.
5. **Non-Maleficence:** Avoid decisions that cause harm to registrars, practice, or community.
6. **Justice:** Ensure decisions are consistent with practice values and do not create inequitable outcomes.

### Ethical Decision-Making Questions

When facing a conflict, Practice Managers should ask:

1. **Transparency:** Have I been honest with all stakeholders about the constraints and trade-offs?
2. **Fairness:** Does this decision unfairly disadvantage the registrar or any other party?
3. **Accountability:** Can I justify this decision if questioned by TPA, ACRRM, or the registrar?
4. **Beneficence:** Does this decision support the registrar's development and rural retention?
5. **Non-Maleficence:** Does this decision avoid causing harm to the registrar, practice, or community?
6. **Justice:** Is this decision consistent with our practice values and equitable for all parties?

## 8. Conflict Case Studies

The decision tree includes embedded case studies that illustrate how Practice Managers have navigated common conflicts:

### Case Study 1: Teaching Time vs Revenue (Conflict Type 2)

**Scenario:**  
Rural practice with tight financial margins accepts AGPT registrar. Practice Owner pressures Practice Manager to reduce teaching time to maintain revenue.

**Conflict:**  
Training Quality (protected teaching time) vs Business Sustainability (revenue generation)

**Resolution:**
1. Practice Manager conducted honest financial analysis and identified that GP Training Incentive Payments could partially offset lost revenue.
2. Negotiated with Practice Owner to protect minimum 1 hour formal teaching per week, supplemented by informal teaching during clinical sessions.
3. Engaged TPA to access Flexible Funds for locum coverage during intensive teaching periods (e.g., exam preparation).
4. Monitored registrar feedback and adjusted teaching time as needed.

**Outcome:**  
Registrar received adequate teaching support, practice maintained financial viability, and registrar completed training successfully and joined practice as partner post-Fellowship.

**Lessons Learned:**
- Transparent financial analysis helped Practice Owner understand trade-offs
- Accessing available funding (Incentive Payments, Flexible Funds) reduced financial pressure
- Long-term workforce planning (registrar as future partner) justified short-term revenue loss

---

### Case Study 2: NTCER Compliance vs Registrar Preferences (Conflict Type 6)

**Scenario:**  
Registrar requests flexible part-time employment arrangement that does not align with NTCER-mandated full-time employment terms.

**Conflict:**  
NTCER Compliance (mandatory full-time employment for AGPT) vs Registrar Experience (preference for flexibility)

**Resolution:**
1. Practice Manager explained NTCER requirements to registrar and clarified that part-time arrangements are not compliant for AGPT pathway.
2. Engaged TPA to confirm compliance requirements and provide registrar with official explanation.
3. Explored alternative pathways (e.g., RGTS) that may allow part-time arrangements, but registrar was committed to AGPT.
4. Registrar accepted full-time employment with understanding that compliance protects their training and funding eligibility.

**Outcome:**  
Registrar accepted NTCER-compliant employment terms after understanding rationale. Practice maintained compliance and accreditation.

**Lessons Learned:**
- Clear communication about compliance rationale reduced registrar resistance
- TPA support provided external validation and credibility
- Exploring alternatives demonstrated practice's willingness to accommodate where possible

## 9. Conflict Recognition Visual Indicators

The decision tree uses visual indicators to alert Practice Managers to potential conflicts:

### Visual Indicator System

- ⚠️ **Conflict Warning Icon:** Appears at decision nodes where competing priorities are likely to clash
- 🔮 **Conflict Anticipation Icon:** Appears at decision nodes where conflicts can be prevented with proactive action
- ⚖️ **Trade-Off Required Icon:** Appears when Practice Manager must choose between competing priorities
- 🆘 **Escalation Recommended Icon:** Appears when conflict requires external support or mediation

### Color-Coded Conflict Severity

- 🟡 **Yellow:** Low-severity conflict; can typically be resolved with internal discussion and compromise
- 🟠 **Orange:** Medium-severity conflict; may require stakeholder engagement or external guidance
- 🔴 **Red:** High-severity conflict; requires escalation to TPA, ACRRM, or external mediation
