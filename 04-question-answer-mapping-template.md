## Executive Summary

This document establishes the **standardized Question-Answer Mapping Template** for each decision node in the ACRRM Practice Manager Decision Tree. This template serves as the interface into which handbook content, NTCER 2026.1 clauses, NotebookLM-developed quizzes, and practice scenarios will be populated. The template ensures consistency, comprehensiveness, and usability across all decision nodes.

**Critical Design Principle:** Every decision node must provide Practice Managers with clear questions, evidence-based answers, consequence frameworks, and stakeholder engagement guidance. The template is designed to be populated systematically, ensuring no critical information is omitted.

|------------------|------------------------|--------------------------|
| 1. Decision Context | Handbook restructure document | Practice Manager interviews |

| 2.2 Training Quality | ACRRM curriculum, AFEP programme | Supervision standards |
| 2.3 Business Sustainability | Funding guidelines | Practice financial models |

| 3. Conflict Recognition | Conflict Recognition Architecture (Doc 03) | Practice Manager case studies |
| 4. Decision Options | Handbook content | NotebookLM-developed quizzes |
| 5. Stakeholder Engagement | Stakeholder mapping | Communication best practices |
| 6. Escalation Pathways | TPA/ACRRM protocols | Mediation frameworks |

| 10. FAQs | Common Practice Manager questions | TPA/ACRRM support logs |
| 11. Additional Resources | Handbook appendices | External resource databases |

### 2.2 Content Quality Standards

All content populated into the template must meet the following quality standards:

**Clarity:**
- Use plain language, avoiding jargon where possible
- Define technical terms when first introduced
- Use active voice and direct language

**Accuracy:**
- Verify all NTCER clauses against official NTCER 2026.1 document
- Cross-reference handbook content with ACRRM curriculum
- Validate funding information with current guidelines

**Completeness:**
- Ensure all mandatory template fields are populated
- Provide sufficient detail for Practice Managers to make informed decisions
- Include all relevant stakeholder perspectives

**Consistency:**
- Use consistent terminology across all decision nodes
- Maintain consistent formatting and structure
- Ensure cross-references are accurate and bidirectional

**Usability:**
- Organize information in logical, scannable format
- Use bullet points, checklists, and tables where appropriate
- Provide clear calls-to-action and next steps

### 2.3 Template Customization

While the template provides a standardized structure, certain sections may be customized based on decision node type:

**High-Complexity Nodes (e.g., Employment Compliance):**
- Expand "Decision Options" section with more detailed step-by-step processes
- Include additional case studies to illustrate nuances
- Provide more extensive FAQ section

**High-Conflict Nodes (e.g., Teaching Time Protection):**
- Expand "Conflict Recognition" section with detailed trade-off analysis
- Include more stakeholder engagement scripts
- Provide additional ethical decision-making guidance

**High-Frequency Nodes (e.g., Onboarding Checklist):**
- Simplify "Decision Context" for quick reference
- Provide downloadable checklists and templates
- Include quick-start guides

**Low-Complexity Nodes (e.g., Simple Resource Links):**
- Condense template to essential sections only
- Focus on "Decision Options" and "Additional Resources"
- Minimize conflict recognition if not applicable

## 3. CONFLICT RECOGNITION

### 3.1 Conflict Detection

**Conflict Present:** Yes

**Conflict Type:** Type 1 (Compliance vs Business Sustainability) - Minor conflict  
**Competing Priorities:** Layer 1 (Compliance) vs Layer 3 (Business Sustainability)  
**Conflict Severity:** Low

### 3.2 Tension Analysis

**Why These Priorities Conflict:**
Both GPSA and GPRA templates are NTCER-compliant, but they have different implications for practice administrative burden and financial control. Practices must choose between reduced administrative burden (GPSA) and greater employment control (GPRA).

**Typical Manifestations:**
- Practice Owner prefers GPRA for control over employment terms, but Practice Manager concerned about payroll complexity
- Practice prefers GPSA for simplicity, but wants more flexibility in rostering than GPSA template allows

**Stakeholder Perspectives:**
- **Registrar:** Generally neutral; both templates are compliant and protect registrar rights
- **Practice Manager:** May prefer GPSA for reduced administrative burden
- **Practice Owner:** May prefer GPRA for greater control over employment terms
- **TPA/ACRRM:** Neutral; both templates are acceptable if used correctly

### 3.3 Trade-Off Framework

**Option 1: Prioritize Administrative Simplicity (Choose GPSA Template)**
- ✅ Consequences for Business Sustainability: Reduced administrative burden, simpler payroll
- ⚠️ Consequences for Business Sustainability: Less control over employment terms, must accept GPSA-determined salary
- **When to Choose:** Practice has limited administrative capacity or prefers to outsource payroll management

**Option 2: Prioritize Employment Control (Choose GPRA Template)**
- ✅ Consequences for Business Sustainability: Greater control over employment terms, flexibility in salary negotiation
- ⚠️ Consequences for Business Sustainability: Increased administrative burden, practice manages payroll
- **When to Choose:** Practice has administrative capacity and prefers direct employment relationship

**Option 3: Escalate for External Support**
- Not applicable for this decision; both options are compliant. Choice is based on practice preference.

### 3.4 Ethical Considerations

**Transparency:** Explain to registrar which template is being used and why  
**Fairness:** Both templates are fair; choice does not disadvantage registrar  
**Accountability:** Document which template was chosen and rationale

## 4. DECISION OPTIONS

### Option 1: Use GPSA Employment Template

**Description:**
GPSA (General Practice Supervisors Australia) employs the registrar directly. Practice pays top-up (if applicable) but does not manage payroll or employment administration.

**Step-by-Step Process:**
1. Download GPSA employment template from [link]
2. Complete practice-specific sections (practice name, supervisor details, rostering arrangements)
3. Do NOT modify mandatory NTCER clauses
4. Provide template to registrar for review
5. Arrange signing (practice representative, registrar, GPSA representative)
6. Provide signed copy to TPA
7. Coordinate with GPSA for payroll commencement

**Multi-Layer Consequences:**

**Layer 1 (Compliance):**
- Immediate: ✓ Fully NTCER-compliant, meets TPA requirements
- Long-term: ✓ Ongoing compliance maintained by GPSA

**Layer 2 (Training Quality):**
- Immediate: ✓ Teaching time protected by NTCER clauses
- Long-term: ✓ Supervisor obligations clearly defined

**Layer 3 (Business Sustainability):**
- Immediate: ✓ Reduced administrative burden (no payroll management)
- Long-term: ✓ Lower administrative costs, but less employment control

**Layer 4 (Registrar Experience):**
- Immediate: ✓ Registrar has national employer (GPSA), may provide stability
- Long-term: ✓ Clear employment terms support positive experience

**Overall Risk Rating:** Low

**Recommended When:**
- Practice has limited administrative capacity
- Practice prefers to outsource payroll management
- Practice is comfortable with GPSA-determined salary and terms

**Required Resources:**
- GPSA employment template
- Practice details (ABN, contact information)
- Supervisor accreditation details

**Templates/Tools:**
- [GPSA Employment Template](link)
- [GPSA Onboarding Guide](link)

---

### Option 2: Use GPRA Employment Template

**Description:**
GPRA (General Practice Registrars Australia) template is used when practice employs registrar directly. Practice manages payroll and receives funding reimbursement.

**Step-by-Step Process:**
1. Download GPRA employment template from [link]
2. Complete practice-specific sections (practice name, salary, rostering arrangements)
3. Do NOT modify mandatory NTCER clauses
4. Provide template to registrar for review
5. Arrange signing (practice representative, registrar)
6. Provide signed copy to TPA
7. Set up payroll system for registrar (including superannuation, tax withholding)
8. Apply for funding reimbursement via PRODA/HPOS

**Multi-Layer Consequences:**

**Layer 1 (Compliance):**
- Immediate: ✓ Fully NTCER-compliant, meets TPA requirements
- Long-term: ✓ Ongoing compliance maintained if practice adheres to NTCER terms

**Layer 2 (Training Quality):**
- Immediate: ✓ Teaching time protected by NTCER clauses
- Long-term: ✓ Supervisor obligations clearly defined

**Layer 3 (Business Sustainability):**
- Immediate: ⚠️ Increased administrative burden (practice manages payroll)
- Long-term: ✓ Greater control over employment terms and rostering, but higher administrative costs

**Layer 4 (Registrar Experience):**
- Immediate: ✓ Registrar has direct employment relationship with practice
- Long-term: ✓ Clear employment terms support positive experience

**Overall Risk Rating:** Low (if practice has administrative capacity)

**Recommended When:**
- Practice has administrative capacity to manage payroll
- Practice prefers direct employment relationship with registrar
- Practice wants greater control over employment terms and rostering

**Required Resources:**
- GPRA employment template
- Payroll system (or accountant support)
- PRODA/HPOS access for funding claims

**Templates/Tools:**
- [GPRA Employment Template](link)
- [Payroll Setup Guide for Registrars](link)
- [PRODA/HPOS Access Guide](link)

## 10. FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

**Q1: Can I modify the GPSA or GPRA template to suit my practice's needs?**  
A: No. The GPSA and GPRA templates contain mandatory NTCER 2026.1 clauses that cannot be modified or removed. You can complete practice-specific sections (e.g., rostering arrangements) but cannot alter mandatory clauses. Modifying mandatory clauses will result in non-compliance and funding ineligibility.

**Q2: What's the difference between GPSA and GPRA employment?**  
A: GPSA employment means the registrar is employed by General Practice Supervisors Australia (GPSA), with your practice as the training site. GPSA manages payroll, and your practice may pay a top-up. GPRA employment means your practice employs the registrar directly using the GPRA template, and you manage payroll and claim funding reimbursement. Both are NTCER-compliant; the choice depends on your administrative capacity and preference for employment control.

**Q3: Do I need to use GPSA or GPRA template for all registrar pathways?**  
A: GPSA and GPRA templates are mandatory for AGPT and RGTS 2026+ pathways. Other pathways may have different requirements. Check with your TPA to confirm which template is required for your specific registrar.

**Q4: What happens if I use a non-compliant employment contract?**  
A: Using a non-NTCER-compliant contract will result in:
- Funding ineligibility (no GP Training Incentive Payments or other funding)
- TPA may withdraw registrar placement
- Accreditation at risk if pattern of non-compliance emerges
- Legal liability if registrar employment rights are not protected

**Q5: Can I negotiate salary with the registrar?**  
A: If using GPSA template, salary is determined by GPSA (you cannot negotiate). If using GPRA template, you can negotiate salary within NTCER-compliant ranges, but must meet minimum base rate requirements. Check NTCER 2026.1 for current base rates.
