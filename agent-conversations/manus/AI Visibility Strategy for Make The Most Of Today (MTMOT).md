## 2. The New Search Landscape: From SEO to AEO

The paradigm of online search is undergoing its most significant transformation since the advent of Google. The rise of Large Language Models (LLMs) has created a new information ecosystem where users increasingly seek direct answers from AI, rather than a list of links. This necessitates a strategic evolution from traditional Search Engine Optimisation (SEO) to **Answer Engine Optimisation (AEO)**, also known as Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO).

> Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) is the practice of structuring content so that LLMs like ChatGPT can understand, reference and recommend your brand in response to user questions. [1]

As one industry report notes, an estimated 25% of organic search traffic is projected to shift to AI chatbots and other virtual agents by 2026 [2]. This shift is creating a "zero-click" environment where brand visibility within the AI-generated answer is paramount. The following table summarises the key distinctions between these two disciplines:

| Feature | Traditional SEO | Answer Engine Optimisation (AEO) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Platform** | Google, Bing (Search Engines) | ChatGPT, Meta AI, Perplexity (Answer Engines) |
| **Core Objective** | Achieve high ranking in search results (SERPs) | Be cited or mentioned in AI-generated answers |
| **User Interaction** | Keyword-based queries | Conversational, question-based prompts |
| **Success Metric** | Organic traffic, rankings, impressions | Mentions, citations, referral quality, share of voice |
| **Content Focus** | Optimising pages for keywords | Structuring content to directly answer questions |

For MTMOT, this means our goal is not simply to rank for "executive coaching." It is to become the cited authority when a user asks Meta AI, "How can our company support a senior leader returning to work after a stroke?"

## 6. Answer Engine Optimisation (AEO) Blueprint

To ensure MTMOT is the answer provided by AI, we must structure our content meticulously. Meta AI, like other LLMs, builds its responses by synthesising information from sources it deems authoritative and clear [3].

### 6.1. Target AI Queries

Our content will be optimised to answer high-intent questions from our target market, such as:

*   *"How can companies retain senior executives after a serious illness?"*
*   *"What is the business impact of losing institutional knowledge from departing leaders?"*
*   *"Best practices for supporting an executive returning to work after cancer."
*   *"How can insurers reduce long-term disability costs for high-earning professionals?"*

### 6.2. Content Structure for AI Citation

Every piece of content published on the MTMOT blog will follow a strict structure designed for AI consumption:

1.  **Direct Answer First:** The first paragraph will provide a clear, concise answer to the target question.
2.  **Provide Evidence:** Subsequent paragraphs will include data, statistics, and case study metrics to support the answer.
3.  **Establish E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness):** We will prominently feature Carla’s credentials, personal story, and professional achievements.
4.  **Use Structured Data:** We will implement schema markup (FAQPage, Article, Person, Course) to give AI crawlers explicit context about our content.
5.  **Optimise for Crawlability:** A clean site architecture, fast load times, and a comprehensive XML sitemap will ensure both Bing’s crawler (used by Meta AI) and Meta’s own `Meta-ExternalAgent` can efficiently index our site [3].
